Section 01 — Demo Day Vision
What we're building
What the product delivers — end to end

A user uploads a 30–60 second Goblet Squat video and receives a detailed form analysis and comparison against their previous session with personalised coaching to improve their form and weight-related input.

Results screen — initial analysis
Results screen — form comparison
1  —  Detailed Analysis of Their Session
a
Visual proof of their form — a video or image of them doing the exercise, with joint angles visible on their own body. Seeing yourself is more powerful than any score.
b
Numerical score /100 — an overall form score across the session.
c
1–2 sentence personalised summary — combines their form quality with the weight they were lifting. e.g. "At 12kg your posture holds well across all reps, but your ankle dorsiflexion limits your depth from rep 5 onwards."
d
4 Parameters — Posture · Stability · Movement Quality · Tempo
Each parameter shows four things:
ScoreA score /100 for that parameter
What they're doing well1 sentence affirmation
What's off1 sentence specific observation
Action to work on1 concrete correction
e
Rep-by-rep score chart — visualises how form holds or deteriorates from the first rep to the last. Shows where fatigue or technique breakdown begins.
2  —  Form Comparison Against Previous Session
The longitudinal differentiator
Side-by-side comparison of the user's current session vs their previous session — on the same 4 parameters (Posture, Stability, Movement Quality, Tempo). Shows whether form improved, held, or degraded at the same or different weight. This is what no competitor offers — not a single-session snapshot, but a progression story over time.
👁
Visual proof beats a score
Showing someone their own body at the worst moment — knee caving in, back leaning forward — is more powerful than any number. The annotated frame is the product moment.
📈
Longitudinal, not snapshot
Competitors show one rep. Kinetic shows the journey — how form changes as weight increases, rep by rep, session by session. That's the moat.
⚖️
Weight-specific advice
"Your form at 12kg is solid — try 14kg" or "Your tempo degrades after rep 5 at 16kg." The recommendation is tied to load, not just generic form cues.
Section 02 — What Does Success Look Like
5 things we need to nail for demo day
These are the criteria we evaluate the demo against — not features, but outcomes
01
Pipeline completes in 30–35 seconds
Upload video → annotated results on screen in under 35 seconds. Not a progress bar spinning forever. Measured wall-clock from upload submit to Results screen fully loaded. This proves the product is usable, not just technically functional.
02
Visual proof on the Results screen
The worst-rep bottom frame appears — the user's own body, annotated with actual joint angles (knee, hip, ankle) and gold standard comparison. The image is clear enough to see the form fault. This is the moment the product becomes real to someone who's never seen it before.
03
MediaPipe angle outputs are accurate
The biomechanics data is trustworthy — knee angle, hip angle, and dorsiflexion values on screen match what a trained eye would observe from the same video. Validated by running 3–5 gold standard videos through the pipeline and comparing outputs to known good values. If the foundation is wrong, everything above it is wrong.
04
Weight-specific coaching output
The coaching paragraph references the weight used and links it to what was observed. e.g. "At 12kg your tempo is consistent across reps, but your back angle degrades noticeably after rep 6 — this is typical as load approaches your stability limit. At 14kg this is likely to get worse before it improves." Generic advice ("work on your form") is a fail.
05
Dashboard tells the progression story
The dashboard shows 3–4 sessions of history for a demo user — form score trending up, weight increasing over time, parameter-level breakdowns. A viewer should be able to see the story without explanation: this person is getting stronger and their form is holding. The longitudinal data differentiator needs to be visible, not described.

Section 05 — Scope Decisions
What we kept, simplified, and dropped — and why
Every decision made to reduce complexity without reducing demo value
Simplified
RAG — Vector DB → MD files in system prompt
Originally: Pinecone/Weaviate vector DB · embedding model · ingestion pipeline · retrieval endpoint · chunking strategy.
Now: 5–10 curated coaching documents converted to MD files, injected directly into Claude's system prompt.
Why
1 exercise + <10 documents = vector DB is overkill at this scale. The RAG purpose is coaching language grounding, not biomechanics analysis. System prompt injection achieves the same outcome with a fraction of the build effort. Can scale to vector DB when exercises multiply.
Dropped
Authentication — JWT + Supabase Auth
Originally: sign up, login, JWT middleware, Supabase Auth, session management, auth screens on frontend.
Why
Judges and users are not evaluating login flows. Adds significant Squad 1 + Squad 2 build effort with zero demo value. Replaced with 2–3 hardcoded user IDs seeded in the DB — form comparison, session history, and dashboard all work as expected in a controlled demo environment.
Simplified
User identity — hardcoded demo users with dropdown
Originally: authenticated user_id from JWT token per request.
Now: 2–3 user IDs seeded in DB (user_001, user_002, user_003). Frontend dropdown lets demo operator switch users to show different progression histories.
Why
Gives the demo full control — switch to user_002 to show a user whose form is degrading at higher weights, switch to user_003 to show a strong progression story. More compelling than a single user with limited history.
Dropped
Profile screen backend + Auth screens
Originally: full profile/preferences backend endpoints, login + signup screens built and connected to auth system.
Why
Profile screen exists as a frontend shell (shows dummy data, is routable, looks complete). Auth screens removed entirely — demo starts at Upload. Neither is part of the core product value being evaluated. Post-demo additions.
